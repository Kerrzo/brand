<ArticleSection id="the-question">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">The question</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">I'd seen a still image turned into a cinematic camera move. I wanted to know how much of it I could reproduce locally.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">
      The inspiration came from a video by <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GJxchJkk4Lk&t=329s" class="inline-link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Jason Lee</a>. He used Higgsfield to turn generated architectural images into <strong>cinematic camera moves</strong> for the web.
    </p>
    <p class="body-copy">I loved the result. My immediate question was: could I get somewhere similar using <strong>local AI</strong> instead?</p>
    <p class="body-copy">That question became <strong>Vesper</strong> — a fictional cabin-rental site simple enough that I could focus on the <strong>production workflow</strong> rather than the product.</p>
    <div>
      <Button href="/sandbox/vesper/" variant="outline" newTab>View the Vesper experiment &rarr;</Button>
    </div>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection dark tight id="the-approach">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label" dark>The approach</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">From prompt to page.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">I didn't copy the workflow. I changed one assumption: <strong>run the generation locally</strong>.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">I connected Claude Code to ComfyUI through MCP, with the models running <strong>locally on my machine</strong>.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Claude could work on the site and orchestrate ComfyUI from the same coding environment.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">That was more interesting than MCP itself: code, prompts, generated assets and the website had become <strong>one production environment</strong>.</p>
    <div class="section-spacing-top">
      <ArticlePipeline dark steps={["Claude Code", "MCP", "ComfyUI", "Local model", "Vesper"]} />
    </div>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection tight id="the-engine">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">The engine</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">What was actually generating the movement?</h2>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>Give it a still image and a camera instruction and it can generate a remarkably convincing short sequence. But there is no camera. And there is no 3D cabin behind the image.</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy">I chained several of those sequences together, using the end of one as the starting point for the next, then assembled them into one continuous journey.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">That distinction became the whole experiment: the model wasn't travelling through a cabin. It was repeatedly inventing what might exist beyond the frame.</p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection dark id="it-worked">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label" dark>The result</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">And for a while, the illusion worked.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">Small camera moves worked. Atmospheric movement worked. Some of the individual transitions looked fantastic.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Put them into the website and the effect was <strong>surprisingly convincing</strong>.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Then I asked the camera to travel further. That's where it got interesting.</p>

<ArticleSection id="mental-model">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">What I'd change</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">Next time, I'll design the space before generating the journey.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">Next time I'd start with the world, not the image: a <strong>floorplan</strong> or simple <strong>3D blockout</strong>, the relationship between rooms, and the path I want the camera to travel.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Then I'd let AI generate around those constraints. It can still invent the pixels. It just doesn't have to invent the <strong>architecture</strong> at the same time.</p>
