<ArticleSection id="the-question">
  <SectionLabel>The question</SectionLabel>

  <ArticleStatement size="lg" class="section-spacing-top">
    I'd seen AI turn still images into beautiful cinematic experiences. I wanted to know how much of that I could reproduce locally.
  </ArticleStatement>

  <div class="section-spacing-top narrow-copy">
    <p class="body-copy">I wasn't particularly interested in making another AI-generated website.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">The interesting question was whether a local workflow could produce the kind of controlled, cinematic motion I'd seen created with hosted tools such as Higgsfield.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Could I generate the visual world, create the movement, direct the transitions and turn the result into something that actually felt designed?</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Vesper became the experiment. It is a fictional cabin-rental experience: deliberately visual, atmospheric and simple enough that I could concentrate on the production workflow rather than the product itself.</p>

<ArticleSection id="the-discovery">
  <ArticleStatement size="xl">A beautiful image isn't a world.</ArticleStatement>

  <div class="section-spacing-top narrow-copy">
    <p class="body-copy">A still image only needs to describe what the camera can see. The moment I asked the camera to travel through the scene, the model had a much harder problem.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">The cabin looked coherent from one viewpoint, but there was no real cabin behind the image. There was no floorplan. No persistent geometry. No authoritative answer to what should exist around the corner.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">As the generated camera travelled further, the model increasingly had to invent the space itself. Doors could lead somewhere unexpected. Rooms could subtly change. Geometry could drift. A convincing image could become a less convincing place.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">This was the point where the experiment became much more useful than simply producing a nice website.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="section-spacing-top">

</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection id="mental-model">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">What I'd change</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">Next time, I'd design the space before generating the journey.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">My first instinct was: <strong>image &rarr; motion</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy">My next attempt would start further upstream: <strong>space &rarr; viewpoints &rarr; camera path &rarr; key frames &rarr; motion</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy">Rather than asking a video model to continuously invent what exists beyond the frame, I would first establish more of the world. That might begin with a floorplan or simple 3D spatial model. From that I could define rooms, relationships between spaces, important viewpoints and an intentional camera path.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">AI can then generate or enhance imagery around a structure that already exists. The model is still doing creative work. It just has fewer architectural decisions to hallucinate.</p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>

<ArticleSection tight id="local-ai">
  <h3 class="h-3">Local was not the limitation I expected.</h3>
  <div class="section-spacing-top narrow-copy">
    <p class="body-copy">One reason for doing the experiment was simply to see whether I needed the hosted tools that originally inspired it. For this experiment, I didn't.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">ComfyUI gave me enough control to explore the workflow locally, iterate freely and connect generation into the rest of the build process.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">That doesn't mean local is always better. Hosted models may produce better results, expose different capabilities or remove significant setup work.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">But the experiment changed the question for me from &ldquo;Can local AI do this?&rdquo; to &ldquo;Which parts of this workflow actually benefit from being local?&rdquo;</p>
    <p class="body-copy">That is a much more useful question.</p>
  </div>
</ArticleSection>
