<ArticleSection id="the-question">
  <SectionLabel>The question</SectionLabel>

  <ArticleStatement size="lg" class="section-spacing-top">
    I'd seen a still image turned into a cinematic camera move. I wanted to know how much of that I could reproduce locally.
  </ArticleStatement>

  <div class="section-spacing-top narrow-copy">
    <p class="body-copy">
      I first saw the idea in a video by <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GJxchJkk4Lk&t=329s" class="inline-link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Jason Lee</a>. He was using Higgsfield to turn generated architectural imagery into cinematic camera movements for the web.
    </p>
    <p class="body-copy">I loved the result. But it immediately gave me another question: could I get somewhere similar without the hosted workflow?</p>
    <p class="body-copy">That question became Vesper — a fictional cabin-rental experience, deliberately visual and simple enough that I could concentrate on the production workflow rather than the product itself.</p>

<ArticleSection tight id="the-approach">
  <SectionLabel>The approach</SectionLabel>
  <h2 class="h-2 section-spacing-top">From prompt to page.</h2>

  <div class="section-spacing-top narrow-copy">
    <p class="body-copy">I didn't rebuild the workflow I'd seen. I changed one of its assumptions.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Instead of using a hosted creative platform, I connected Claude Code to ComfyUI through MCP and explored how much of the same process I could run locally.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">That meant I could work from the coding environment while Claude orchestrated generation through ComfyUI.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">The interesting part wasn't MCP itself. It was that code, prompts, generated assets and the website were beginning to feel like one production environment.</p>
  </div>

<ArticleSection tight id="the-engine">
  <SectionLabel>The engine</SectionLabel>
  <h2 class="h-2 section-spacing-top">What was actually generating the movement?</h2>

  <div class="section-spacing-top narrow-copy">
    <p class="body-copy">The camera movement in Vesper comes from Wan2.2, a local video generation model running through ComfyUI on my own hardware.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Wan2.2 takes a still image and generates a short clip of plausible motion from it — not by moving a virtual camera through a 3D scene, but by predicting frames that plausibly follow on from the one it started with.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Vesper's flythrough is built from several of these clips generated in sequence, each one picking up from where the last left off, then stitched together into a single continuous journey.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">That distinction matters: the model was never navigating a real cabin. It was generating a plausible next frame, conditioned on what came before.</p>
  </div>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection dark id="it-worked">
  <SectionLabel dark>The result</SectionLabel>
  <h2 class="h-2 section-spacing-top">And for a while, the illusion worked.</h2>

  <div class="section-spacing-top narrow-copy">
    <p class="body-copy">A strong source image and a good video model can create remarkably convincing movement. Small camera pushes worked. Atmospheric movement worked. Individual transitions could look beautiful.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Placed inside the website, it felt more expensive and cinematic than the effort involved would suggest.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">That alone was useful. But pushing the camera further exposed a much more interesting limitation.</p>
  </div>

<ArticleSection id="the-discovery">
  <ArticleStatement size="xl">A beautiful image isn't a world.</ArticleStatement>

  <div class="section-spacing-top narrow-copy">
    <p class="body-copy">A still image only has to explain what the camera can see. Move the camera and the problem changes.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">There was no cabin behind my cabin. No floorplan. No persistent geometry. No authoritative answer to what existed around the corner.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">The model could generate a convincing journey through the image, but the further I asked it to travel, the more of the world it had to invent.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">That is where the illusion started to break.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="section-spacing-top">
    <ArticleMedia label="SOURCE FRAME → GENERATED CAMERA MOVEMENT → SPATIAL DRIFT" caption="I will provide real frames later." ratio="21/9" />
  </div>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection id="mental-model">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">What I'd change</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">Next time, I'll design the space before generating the journey.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">Rather than asking a video model to continuously invent what exists beyond the frame, I would first establish more of the world — a floorplan or a simple 3D spatial model, rooms, relationships between spaces, and an intentional camera path.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">AI can then generate or enhance imagery around a structure that already exists. The model is still doing creative work. It just has fewer architectural decisions to hallucinate.</p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>

<ArticleSection tight id="local-ai">
  <h3 class="h-3">Local was not the limitation I expected.</h3>
  <div class="section-spacing-top narrow-copy">
    <p class="body-copy">I started this experiment wondering whether local AI could reproduce an idea I'd seen built with hosted tools.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">It could get much closer than I expected.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">The more interesting limitation turned out not to be where the model was running. It was what the model knew about the world it was moving through.</p>
