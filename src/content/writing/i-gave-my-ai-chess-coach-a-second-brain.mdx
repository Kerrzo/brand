<ArticleSection dark id="the-experiment">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">01 / The experiment</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">I wasn't looking for a chess application.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">I've been experimenting with local AI models on an NVIDIA RTX 5090, running them under WSL.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">One was a 2-billion-parameter Jev-Style decision model. Instead of generating a conversational answer, it evaluates a set of possible actions and ranks them.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">I wanted to see what it could do.</p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>Then I thought about <a href="/building/brilliancy/">Brilliancy</a>.</strong></p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection id="the-question">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">02 / The question</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">What should a chess coach do next?</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">Imagine a player rated 920 who keeps missing knight forks. They've made the same mistake several times. Their mastery of the pattern is poor, and they have exercises due for review.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Should the coach explain the mistake? Give another puzzle? Schedule practice? Switch activities? Or simply let them continue?</p>
    <p class="body-copy">I gave the model the player's context and five possible coaching actions. Then I changed the scenario to a player rated 1250 with strong fork mastery.</p>
    <div class="decision-results">
      <table>
        <caption>Two player contexts / Model-assigned probabilities</caption>
        <thead><tr><th scope="col">Action</th><th scope="col">Player A<span>920 · Struggling with forks</span></th><th scope="col">Player B<span>1250 · Strong fork mastery</span></th></tr></thead>

          <tr><th scope="row">Explain</th><td><strong>40.0%</strong></td><td>3.1%</td></tr>

    <p class="body-copy">The model ranked different actions for different player contexts. That was interesting. It didn't establish that either recommendation was right.</p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection dark id="a-better-test">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">03 / A better test</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">Then I changed just the learning history.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">The first two scenarios differed in several ways. I wanted to know whether the model was responding to the player's learning history.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">In a more controlled test, the chess situation and rating stayed the same. Only the player's mastery and history changed.</p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>The model changed its recommended action in response.</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy">That gave me a more specific observation to follow up: the same chess situation could lead to a different coaching choice depending on what the player had already learned.</p>

  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">04 / Real evidence</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">A decision in milliseconds.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">The playground lets me inspect the session state, the candidate actions and the raw response from the local model.</p>
    <div class="playground-evidence">
      <a href="/media/writing/second-brain/jev-playground.webp" target="_blank" rel="noopener" aria-label="Open the full-resolution Jev Playground screenshot">

    <p class="body-copy"><strong>The model ranked “explain” first. That does not mean the system should automatically act on it.</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy">In this example, the gate blocks the selected action because the distribution does not meet its configured confidence thresholds. Ranking an option and deciding whether to act are separate decisions.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">These are model-assigned probabilities. They aren't calibrated confidence in a coaching decision, or evidence that the advice will help someone learn.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">This screenshot is a separate run showing 145 ms total execution time. The earlier 13–14 ms figures are decision measurements; they aren't interchangeable with that total.</p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection dark id="the-connection">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">05 / The connection</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">What if Brilliancy had a coaching brain?</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">The experiment suggested an architecture that combines several specialised capabilities rather than asking one model to do everything.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">An LLM could interpret the game, plan the coaching and explain it. Jev-Style could help rank the possible interventions. Both would need verified chess evidence and a useful picture of the player.</p>
    <div class="coaching-diagram"><CoachingArchitecture /></div>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection id="the-next-question">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">06 / The next question</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">Would it actually make someone a better chess player?</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">The early results are encouraging. But selecting a plausible action isn't the same as improving someone's learning.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">The next test is whether a coaching system using this decision model makes better choices than a simpler LLM-led approach.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">That means testing against real games, real player histories and eventually real learners.</p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>The architecture is a hypothesis, not a finished Brilliancy feature.</strong></p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection dark id="closing">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">07 / Closing</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">An experiment found somewhere to go.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">I started the morning testing a model I didn't have a use for.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">A few tests later, I had a new idea for Brilliancy.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Whether it helps someone become a better chess player is still an open question.</p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>That's what I want to test next.</strong></p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>
