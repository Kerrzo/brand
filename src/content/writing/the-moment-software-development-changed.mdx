<ArticleSection dark id="the-opportunity">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">01 / The opportunity</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">We'd wanted to rebuild Strategy Connect for years.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">Strategy Connect was a platform we'd built and maintained over many years.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">The software had evolved, the requirements had changed, and our understanding of the product had grown considerably.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">We knew there were things we'd do differently if we were starting again.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">But rebuilding a working platform is expensive. Months of development, significant investment and the risk of disrupting something customers already depend on.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">There was always something more immediately valuable to work on.</p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>The rebuild made technical sense. It just didn't make economic sense.</strong></p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection id="the-experiment">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">02 / The experiment</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">Then the client suggested we try something different.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">Aidan, the owner of Strategy Connect, knew I'd been experimenting with AI-assisted development.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">We started discussing whether the technology had progressed enough to change the economics of a rebuild.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Rather than committing to a conventional development project, we could try building a new foundation using AI-assisted tools and see how far we got.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">We had years of product knowledge, a clear understanding of the business and plenty of ideas about what we'd change.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">What we didn't know was how much faster this new workflow could make us.</p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>The cost of finding out had become small enough to give it a go.</strong></p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection dark id="a-different-workflow">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">03 / A different workflow</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">The biggest change wasn't the code. It was the feedback loop.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">Traditionally, turning an architectural idea into working software takes time.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">You design the approach, implement it, run it, review the result, make changes and repeat.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">AI-assisted development compressed that cycle considerably.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">I could describe an architectural direction, work through an implementation with the AI, run the software, review what it had produced and immediately refine it.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">The AI handled much of the implementation work, while I concentrated on architecture, product behaviour, integration and deciding whether the result was actually any good.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">It wasn't a case of typing a prompt and walking away.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">There were mistakes, corrections, technical decisions and plenty of iteration.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">But the pace was completely different.</p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>I was spending more time deciding what the software should be and less time translating those decisions into code.</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy">That's when the workflow really started to click for me.</p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection id="the-rebuild">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">04 / The rebuild</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">Work we'd expected to take months was suddenly achievable in weeks.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">We made progress far faster than I would have expected using our previous approach.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">A new technical foundation that had been difficult to justify commercially became practical to build and evaluate.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">And importantly, we weren't starting from nothing.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">We had years of understanding the product, its users, its requirements and the problems we wanted to solve.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">That knowledge mattered enormously.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">AI didn't invent the product or make the architectural decisions for us. It accelerated the process of turning those decisions into working software.</p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>The experience changed my expectations of what a small, experienced team could achieve.</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy">It also changed how I thought about the economics of software development.</p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection dark id="something-unexpected">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">05 / Something unexpected</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">Then Aidan started building too.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">One of the most interesting developments came after the initial rebuild.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Aidan began using AI-assisted development to make changes to the platform himself.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">He isn't a software developer. But he understands Strategy Connect, its customers and the business better than anyone.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Previously, making a change generally meant explaining what he wanted, having a developer implement it, reviewing the result and repeating the process.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Now he could participate much more directly.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">That doesn't mean every change is automatically production-ready or that engineering oversight becomes unnecessary.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">But it does mean someone with deep product knowledge can get much closer to the implementation.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">And I find that fascinating.</p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>AI isn't just accelerating development. It's changing who can participate in creating software.</strong></p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection id="what-changed-for-me">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">06 / What changed for me</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">It feels like a new superpower.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">I've spent decades learning how to design and build software.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">That experience hasn't become less useful. If anything, I've found myself drawing on it more.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Understanding architecture, recognising problems, knowing what to test and being able to judge the quality of an implementation all become important when software can be generated so quickly.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">The difference is the speed at which I can put that experience to work.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">An idea no longer has to wait until I've found the time to implement every detail myself.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">I can explore an approach, build a working version, test it and change direction while the thinking is still fresh.</p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>It's a new level of leverage for someone who already knows how to build software.</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy">And it's made building exciting in a way I haven't felt for a long time.</p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection dark id="the-bigger-opportunity">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">07 / The bigger opportunity</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">Speed changes what becomes worth building.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">For me, the most interesting consequence isn't simply delivering the same software faster.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">It's what happens to ideas that previously weren't worth pursuing.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">A rebuild that was too expensive.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">A feature that would take too long to validate.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">An experiment that needed a team before we could even find out whether it worked.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">When the cost of turning an idea into working software falls dramatically, the calculation changes.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">You can test more ideas. Explore more alternatives. Discover mistakes earlier. Spend less time debating what might work and more time finding out.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Of course, faster implementation doesn't automatically mean better products.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">You still need to understand the problem, make good decisions, test what you've built and put it in front of real users.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">But now we have a much more powerful way of doing that.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">And that opens up possibilities I find genuinely exciting.</p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection id="looking-forward">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">08 / Looking forward</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">I don't want to go back.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">I still enjoy programming. I still care about architecture and engineering.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">But I don't miss spending days implementing something that I can now explore in hours.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">AI-assisted development has become part of how I work, alongside the engineering practices and product judgement I've developed over my career.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">I'm still learning where it works well, where it struggles and how to use it responsibly.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">But the Strategy Connect experience changed my perspective.</p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>We haven't made software engineering irrelevant.</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>We've given ourselves a much more powerful way to practise it.</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy">And I'm only beginning to explore what that makes possible.</p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>
