<ArticleSection dark id="the-beginning">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">01 / The beginning</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">The first time AI surprised me was in the 90s.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">I studied Computer Science at Queen’s University Belfast in the late 90s. One project that stuck with me was an AI and computer vision exercise involving images of four different tanks.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">The tanks appeared in different environments — snow, grass, jungle and sand — and from different orientations. The challenge was to write software that could take an image we hadn’t seen before and identify which tank was in it.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">We had to extract useful information from the image and use it to make a classification. By today’s standards it was primitive, but at the time it blew my mind.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">I’d written software that could <strong>look at something it hadn’t seen before and tell me what it was.</strong></p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection id="thirty-years-later">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">Nearly 30 years later</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">AI could help build the software.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">When the ChatGPT moment happened, I became interested in where AI was heading. AI-assisted development was one of the areas I explored early.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Initially, I wasn’t convinced. The tools were interesting, but the workflow was clunky. I could see the potential, but <strong>I didn’t yet trust it for serious production work.</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy">Then I got an opportunity to test that assumption on a platform I knew well.</p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection dark id="strategy-connect">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">02 / Strategy Connect</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">There was a platform we’d wanted to rebuild for years.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">Years earlier, I’d built a platform for Strategy Connect. It became a production system and continued evolving over many years.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">The code evolved. The features evolved. More importantly, <strong>our understanding of the product evolved.</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy">We'd wanted to rethink its technical foundation, but a conventional rebuild meant months of development and significant cost. There was always something more immediately valuable to spend that time and money on.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">The existing platform worked.</p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>The rebuild just didn't make economic sense.</strong></p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection tight id="the-experiment">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">03 / The experiment</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">Then Aidan asked a different question.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">Aidan, the owner of Strategy Connect, knew I’d been experimenting with AI-assisted development.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Instead of asking what a conventional rebuild would cost, we asked whether AI had changed the economics enough to make it worth trying.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Could we use what we'd learned about the product to create a new foundation with AI-assisted development?</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Neither of us knew exactly how it would go.</p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>The cost of finding out had become small enough that we could try.</strong></p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection dark id="seven-years-to-days">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">04 / The rebuild</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">Then months became days.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">This is where my view of AI-assisted development changed.</p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>A rebuild I would previously have expected to take months took days.</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy">We weren't recreating years of development in a few days. We already had something far more valuable than a blank specification: <strong>years of understanding what the product needed to be.</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy">AI dramatically shortened the distance between that understanding and working software.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">And suddenly a rebuild we'd struggled to justify became practical.</p>
    <div class="foundation-evidence section-spacing-top-lg" aria-label="Months expected, days to a new foundation">

      <p><span class="foundation-evidence__word">Days</span><span class="foundation-evidence__label">to a new foundation</span></p>
    </div>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection id="the-boundary-moved">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">05 / What happened next</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">Then something even more interesting happened.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">Aidan continued developing the platform himself.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">He isn’t a software developer. But <strong>he understands the business and the product deeply.</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy">Historically, someone in his position might describe a change, hand it to a developer, wait for it to be implemented, review it and repeat.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">AI-assisted development gave him a much more direct role in shaping the software.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">That didn't eliminate the need for engineering judgement. But it changed who could participate in building software — and <strong>that interested me as much as the speed of the rebuild.</strong></p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection dark id="what-i-learned">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">What I learned</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">It changed what felt practical to try.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">For most of my career, an idea had to justify a team, a timeline and a development budget before it could become working software.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">That constraint hasn't disappeared.</p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>But the threshold has moved.</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy">Architecture still matters. Product judgement still matters. Engineering still matters. You still need to understand the problem, decide what should exist and assess whether what you've built is any good.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">What changed was how quickly we could turn that thinking into something real enough to test.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">And that changes which ideas are worth trying in the first place.</p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>

<ArticleSection tight id="final-conclusion">
  <ArticleTwoCol>
    <SectionLabel slot="label">Nearly 30 years apart</SectionLabel>
    <h2 class="h-2">AI surprised me again.</h2>
    <p class="body-copy">In the late 90s, I was amazed that I could write software capable of looking at an image it hadn’t seen before and deciding which tank was in it.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">Nearly thirty years later, AI surprised me from the other direction.</p>
    <p class="body-copy"><strong>This time, it was helping me write the software.</strong></p>
    <p class="body-copy">I now approach a rebuild or a new product idea with a different sense of what might be practical.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">The thinking still matters.</p>
    <p class="body-copy">The distance between thinking and building has become dramatically shorter.</p>
  </ArticleTwoCol>
</ArticleSection>
